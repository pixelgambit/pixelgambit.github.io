A roguelike card crawl through a 44-card dungeon. Every room deals you four cards: monsters to fight, weapons to bind and potions to drink. Choose the order well, because the deck does not forgive.

How a run works

You start with 20 health and a shuffled deck. Each room puts four cards on the table, and you must deal with three of them before you can move on. The fourth stays behind and becomes part of the next room.

CardSuitsWhat it does
MonstersSpades and clubsHit you for their rank, 2 up to 14 for an Ace. Fight them with a weapon or with your fists.
WeaponsDiamondsSoak up a monster's damage. Once a weapon kills, it is bound: it can only face monsters no stronger than the last one it beat.
PotionsHeartsHeal you, up to 20. Only one potion works per room, so a second is simply thrown away.

Don't like a room? Escape it before you touch a card, and its cards go to the bottom of the deck. You can't escape two rooms in a row, so pick your moment. Clear the whole deck to win. Your score counts what is left of you, or what is left of the dungeon.

A look inside

What's in it

  • Real playing cards. A classic deck with public-domain court figures and pip layouts, and blue or red backs.
  • A real table. Cards are shuffled, dealt and played like physical cards, with sound, a shake when a blow lands and a buzz in your hand.
  • Pick your table. Green felt, dark wood, or real photographed cloth, wood, stone and leather.
  • Every choice shows its cost. Lift a card and it tells you the health each move leaves you, or warns you it would be fatal.
  • See the dungeon. Tap the deck to see every card still to come, or the discard pile to see what is gone.
  • Hints when you want them. Ask for the best move and the reason for it, or switch on short tips the first time each situation comes up.
  • A report on every run. How it ended, your health room by room, every monster and what killed it. Records keep your bests, your totals and your nemesis.
  • Pick up where you left off. A run in progress is saved as you play.
  • Made for your phone. Landscape and touch first, sized for a thumb, from wide phones to tablets.

No AI-generated art or music

Everything you see and hear in Monster Bound was made by people. The cards are real playing cards: the court figures are Byron Knoll's public-domain designs and the pips are drawn by the game itself. The tables are real photographs of felt, wood, stone and leather. The music is by human composers Darren Curtis and AlkaKrab, and the title is set in Cinzel Decorative, a typeface by Natanael Gama.

How to join the test

The test is open now. Google has approved Monster Bound for testing on Google Play. Join the group, opt in and install: it takes about five minutes.

Before Monster Bound can launch, Google Play needs at least 12 testers to stay opted in for 14 days in a row, so every tester really helps. You'll need an Android phone and the Google account you use for Play. You don't have to win, or even play much: a run now and then is plenty.

  1. Join the testers group

    Join the Pixel Gambit testers Google Group with the same Google account you use on your phone. If you use a different account, Google won't count you as a tester. Already in it for Fastline? Then you're all set for this step.

    Join the group
  2. Opt in on Google Play

    Open the testing page with the same Google account and tap "Become a tester". If it says the app isn't available, wait a few minutes after joining the group and try again.

    Open the testing page
  3. Install Monster Bound from Play

    Download it from the Play Store link on the testing page, then open it at least once.

  4. Keep it installed for 14 days

    Play as much or as little as you like. Uninstalling early means you stop counting as a tester.
